# Genre Comps for Plastic Platoon
### Deconstructions of the closest competitor games, with the one stealable idea from each

*Researched 2026-08. Plastic Platoon context: mobile-web idle lane-battle. Green plastic army men vs tan. A Molder stamps soldiers who auto-fight down the lane; scrap funds upgrades; prestige resets. Rework goal: retention.*

---

## 1. Art of War: Legions (Fastone Games HK) — THE closest comp

Toy-like low-poly armies you arrange on a grid, then watch auto-battle. Structurally this is Plastic Platoon's closest relative: minimal in-battle input, all the play is in army building between fights.

### Structure
- **Campaign ("Battle" mode):** numbered levels from 1 to a cap of **8,000** (after 8,000 you fight snapshots of other players' armies). Levels are 30–60 second auto-battles. The early curve is deliberately trivial — dozens of near-free wins to establish the "one more level" rhythm — with periodic spike levels that force a formation change or troop upgrade rather than pure stat grinding.
- **Formation grid:** troops placed in rows/columns before battle; the grid (max deployable army) **expands as you level up**, so progression is literally "more toy soldiers on the board." Basic meta: tanks front, melee mid, ranged back — complicated by backline-diver units (Rhino Knight charges to the back row, Shadow Ninjas teleport), which force real formation puzzles, not just stat checks.
- **Troops:** rarities Common → Rare → Epic → Legendary. Community guides count **27 Epic + 10 Legendary (~37)** at the top end alone, on top of a common/rare base roster. Troops are either single-unit (1 big figure) or squads (up to 9 figures per deployment slot). Three broad roles (tank / melee / ranged) plus special mechanics (healers, stunners, summoners, backline divers). **Upgrading = merging duplicates** (fusion), and some top troops only come from "temple fusion" — a long-horizon crafting chase.
- **Unlock cadence:** new troops start unlocking meaningfully around campaign levels **40–75**; recruitment is a coin-fed slot-machine wheel (recruit camp), so coins → random troops → merge → place. The gacha-recruit + merge loop is the game's real core compulsion.
- **Heroes:** one hero anchors the army (a second slot comes later). Three rarities (Rare/Epic/Legendary); leveled with hero cards/shards from chests and events; **Legendary heroes unlock an extra skill at level 11**. Heroes provide auras + a battle skill, and whole formation archetypes are built around a specific hero.
- **Idle earnings:** an **idle chest accrues coins/gems hourly and caps at 6 hours (8h for VIP)**; hourly rate scales with campaign progress. This is the classic 2-3-logins-per-day appointment.
- **Mode stack (unlocks progressively):** Daily Quests (quest points → present box), Expedition (chapter-based PvE with level gates), Arena (**20 async PvP battles/day**), Headhunt (round-limited daily missions), Treasure Hunt, clan systems, Honor Hunting / Infinity War boss content.

### Why it retains
- The battle is a **30-second reward event**, not a challenge — losses are diagnosis moments ("re-arrange, re-merge, retry"), never punishing.
- **Three interleaved progressions**: campaign level (unlocks + idle rate), army size (grid expansion), and troop quality (merge tiers). One of the three is always about to pop.
- Idle chest cap + daily quests + 20 arena tickets structure day-2/day-7 return; new troop types keep appearing across the whole first week of play (levels 40–75+).

### The ONE stealable idea
**The expanding formation grid as the core progression fantasy.** Your army isn't a stat — it's a *board that visibly fills with more and better toy soldiers*, and campaign walls are solved by re-arranging the board (formation puzzle), not just by grinding. For Plastic Platoon: give the Molder's output a deployment grid ("the toy box lid" / staging area) that grows with campaign progress, and make walls counter specific arrangements so players tinker instead of churn.

## 2. The Battle Cats (PONOS) — lane battle + roster collection

The 12-year-old genre king of single-lane base-vs-base battles. 80M+ downloads, still running anniversary events past year 11. Everything about its longevity comes from roster-as-toolbox design.

### Structure
- **Campaign:** three main sagas — Empire of Cats, Into the Future, Cats of the Cosmos — each **3 chapters × 48 stages** (~432 main stages), then Legend Stages (thousands of sub-chapter stages) as the true endgame. Chapter completion gives named unit rewards (Moneko after EoC Ch1, Bahamut after the Moon boss) plus feature unlocks (Cat Combos, extra formation slots, new sagas).
- **Difficulty scaling by magnification:** the *same* enemies reappear with a strength multiplier — EoC Ch1 enemies at 100%, **Ch3 at 400%**; unit deploy costs also shift per chapter (Ch1 costs -33%, Ch2/3 higher). Extremely cheap content reuse that still reads as fair ("I know this guy, he's just stronger").
- **Type-counter system (traits):** enemies carry traits — **Red, Black, Floating, Metal, Angel, Alien, Zombie, Relic, Aku, Traitless** — and cats carry anti-trait abilities (massive damage, resist, freeze, slow, knockback). Counters to both of an enemy's traits stack **multiplicatively**. First red boss appears as early as stage 10 (Maldives), teaching "bring the right tool" within session one.
- **Wall levels (deliberate):** the famous **Moon / Teacher Bun Bun** wall at the end of each EoC chapter requires meatshield tactics, cats at level ~20, and most treasures collected — i.e., it's a *checklist wall*: fail → the game hands you a to-do list (farm XP, collect treasures, unlock a counter unit) → return and win. Losses convert directly into session plans.
- **Treasure meta (permanent buffs):** every stage can drop a treasure at **Inferior/Normal/Superior** quality; completing a regional treasure *set* grants **permanent** army-wide buffs (cat HP/attack, wallet efficiency, research speed, anti-Alien debuffs). Full Superior sets award huge XP bonuses (1M XP in EoC, up to 5M in CotC). Limited-time **Treasure Festivals** boost drop odds — an appointment mechanic layered on replaying old content.
- **Unit economy:** normal cats bought and leveled with XP (soft currency); rare/uber units via Cat Food gacha; level caps gated by User Rank and Catseyes; True Forms as long-horizon upgrades. Daily login stamps, weekday rotating farming stages, monthly events, and collabs carry the calendar.

### Why it retains
- The counter system makes every new unit a **key, not a stat stick** — roster growth = capability growth.
- Walls are engineered so that grinding is *directed* (specific treasures, specific counters), which makes D7+ sessions purposeful.
- Treasures make replaying beaten levels the most efficient thing to do — dead content becomes farmland.

### The ONE stealable idea
**Per-stage collectible treasures with quality tiers that pay out permanent army-wide buffs on set completion.** It solves three problems at once: gives replay value to cleared levels, gives losses a to-do list, and gives an appointment hook (festival windows with boosted quality odds). For Plastic Platoon: "Spare Parts" (bronze/silver/gold plastic sprue pieces) dropped per level; complete a theater's set → permanent buff (e.g., +10% all green HP, Molder stamps 5% faster).

## 3. Mob Control (Voodoo) — the count-multiplier crowd fantasy

Voodoo's first hypercasual→hybridcasual conversion (Apr 2021; 50M+ downloads, ~$2M/month revenue, Q1 2025: $7.9M IAP + 21M downloads in the quarter). The reference for making army growth feel exponential *moment to moment*.

### Structure
- **Core loop:** fire units from a cannon through **multiplier gates (x2/x3/x5)** — every unit passing a gate is duplicated that many times — then the swarm crashes into the enemy base. Matches run **1–2 minutes** and are **win-heavy by design**: "in most sessions, players win — and even when they lose, it feels like just a minute or two lost."
- **The gate psychology:** multiplication is *visible and physical* — the crowd doubles before your eyes, and gate choice (which gate, how many units through it) is a skill expression with instant exponential feedback. This is why the mechanic dominates ad creatives: the growth moment is legible in half a second.
- **Hybridization meta (added post-launch):** card collection for units (Chicken, Shotgun, Ninja, etc.), base building, league ladder (Wood → Iron → …), **Adventures mode that restricts which units you may use** (forces roster breadth, prevents one-deck syndrome), and a season framework (missions + reward track).
- **Onboarding pacing (measured):** tutorial ~2 min → lobby/building system ~5 min → first league match ~7 min → Adventures unlock ~46 min → **first paid offer at ~50 min** (only after value is understood). Features drip-unlock continuously.
- **Monetization/ads:** ~85% ads / 15% IAP at conversion; interstitials capped at 1 per 5 matches (≤5s); rewarded ads embedded in the upgrade system; a "Skip It" option claims ad rewards without watching. Development was steered by **D30 and D120 retention metrics**, tested every 2–4 weeks over 9 months.

### Why it retains
- **Density of the core loop** — the analysis's phrase: "bunts, steals, and base hits rather than home runs." A complete emotion cycle (aim → multiply → smash → reward → upgrade) every 90 seconds.
- Winning most matches keeps failure cost near zero; the season/mission frame gives the loop a destination.

### The ONE stealable idea
**Visible mid-lane multiplier gates on your own production stream.** The Molder already stamps soldiers; occasionally route freshly-stamped squads through a player-aimed x2/x3 gate before they hit the lane. It converts passive production into an active, exponential-feeling *moment* — the crowd-doubling dopamine hit inside an idle game — and it's inherently ad-creative-friendly for UA later.

*Sources: [Gamigion analysis](https://www.gamigion.com/mob-control-analysis-how-to-win-the-game-with-a-bunt/), [PocketGamer.biz — hybridising a hypercasual hit](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/comment-and-opinion/79857/mob-control-hybridising-a-hypercasual-hit/), [MAF/Mistplay analysis](https://medium.com/myappfree/mob-control-analysis-the-anatomy-of-a-hybrid-casual-hit-abead6b9fa49), [Reverse Nerf](https://reversenerf.com/voodoos-breakthrough-with-mob-control-their-first-hybridcasual-success/)*

## 4. Grow Castle / Stick War: Legacy — defense idlers with actives + upgrade breadth

### Grow Castle (RaonGames)
- **Structure:** infinite wave defense — castle at left, endless enemy waves, boss waves at intervals. On defeat you drop back some waves and push again; progression is retained (no punishing reset). **120+ heroes**, up to 9 mounted on the castle at once (slot/leadership limited), each with promotable skills; some heroes exist purely to buff the **town archers** (the castle's baseline DPS stat), others provide manual active abilities.
- **Upgrade breadth is the whole game:** parallel gold sinks at different magnitudes — castle HP, town archer level, individual hero levels, hero skill promotions, workers, and **colonies** (map conquest nodes unlocked after wave ~200 that generate passive gold and permanent stat buffs, with a chance to mine crystals). Community rules of thumb — *town archers ≈ 3× castle level, castle ≈ 3× strongest hero* — show how the game trains players to rotate spending across verticals. **Something is always affordable within the next minute of play.**
- **Retention:** guilds + real-time leaderboards, wave-number chasing as a public score, and the colony map as a second slow-burn progression board.

### Stick War: Legacy (Max Games)
- **Structure:** lane RTS-lite — miners generate gold, you build units, and can possess/directly control any single unit (the "be the hero" active layer). Campaign + endless Zombie survival + **Crown of Inamorta tournament mode**: 4-round brackets vs 24 AI opponents where **each round's ruleset is a random mutator** (Classic, Deathmatch — 10,000 starting gold, Super Deathmatch — no miners + instant training, Barricade, Gold Rush — 14 miners/28 deposits). Victories grant upgrade points (mining speed, damage, health); tournament runs start with a pick of unit-buffing avatar portraits (light roguelite flavor).
- **Retention:** the mutator tournament makes the same units feel new weekly; direct control gives a skill-expression valve on top of macro strategy.

### The ONE stealable idea (combined)
**Many parallel upgrade verticals with staggered cost curves, plus a mutator mode.** Grow Castle's lesson: retention in an idle brawler comes from *never having zero affordable buttons* — keep 4–6 upgrade tracks (Molder speed, plastic quality/HP, per-pose damage, base defenses, scrap income) whose costs are deliberately out of phase, so every session ends with a purchase. Stick War's lesson: a weekend tournament with randomized rules (no Molder! double scrap! bazookas only!) re-freshens the same roster for near-zero content cost.

## 5. Egg, Inc. + Leaf Blower Revolution — modern idle progression stacking

### Egg, Inc. (Auxbrain, 2016)
- **Layer stack:** (1) in-run: chickens/habs/vehicles + **common research** (dozens of items across tiers, resets on prestige); (2) **egg tiers** — farm value thresholds promote you to a new egg, restarting the cost curve with a bigger multiplier (a "soft prestige" that re-uses all content); (3) **prestige → Soul Eggs**, each a permanent +10% earnings — and crucially, soul-egg gain has diminishing returns on waiting, so the math *encourages frequent prestiging* rather than hoarding; (4) **Epic Research** (golden eggs, permanent, survives prestige); (5) **Prophecy Eggs** (multiply soul-egg power — earned only from contracts/trophies, i.e., from *playing goals*, not idling); (6) **contracts** — time-limited solo/co-op delivery missions running several days with tiered rewards; (7) **artifacts** — timed rocket missions returning loot to craft permanent equipables.
- **Why "numbers going up" stays satisfying for months:** every layer is a *permanent* bank — no session ends without depositing something into at least one never-resetting account; the exponential fantasy is physically rendered (rivers of chickens); and the layers unlock sequentially over weeks, so the game keeps introducing new meta-currencies right as the previous one's curve flattens.

### Leaf Blower Revolution
- **The maximalist version:** blow leaves → buy upgrades → unlock **new leaf types that are each effectively new currencies** (gold, platinum, exotic, celestial…) → **Prestige** (layer 1: Prestige Coins + shop) → **Big Leaf Crunch** (layer 2: wipes prestige progress, grants BLC Coins **which unlock entire new subsystems** — Science Lab, Volcano, Leaf Tower) → **Mega Leaf Crunch** (layer 3, after beating Tower floor 100) → plus flasks/science, cards (with per-rarity caps to raise), artifacts, borbs + converters (borb juice/tokens), pets, alchemy, crafting, trading, challenges, new areas.
- **The design lesson:** each prestige layer is not just a bigger multiplier — it's a **content gate**. Resetting is how you *unlock new mechanics*, so the reset button is always exciting rather than melancholy. And with a dozen-plus interlocking systems, hitting a wall in one system is the signal to go push a different one — the game never presents a single blocked path.

### Why they retain (D2/D7)
- Egg Inc: prestige is reachable within day 1–2 and immediately visible (+earnings%); contracts create multi-day appointment arcs; rockets create 1–12h check-ins.
- LBR: constant subsystem unlocks — the "what does THAT button do" pull — plus challenges/achievements as directed goals.

### The ONE stealable idea (combined)
**Prestige as a content unlock ladder, not just a multiplier.** First Melt-Down should come fast (day 1–2), pay a visible permanent boost (Egg Inc soul-egg style), and each prestige milestone should switch on a *new subsystem* (research tree → missions → arena → workshop), LBR-style. The reset becomes the most anticipated button in the game.

# Synthesis for Plastic Platoon

The pattern across all five comps: **battles are reward events, not challenges; walls are checklists, not stops; every session banks something permanent; and multiple progression tracks are kept out of phase so a button is always affordable.** Here is how that composes for a toy-soldier idle brawler.

## Campaign / battle structure

- **Theaters, not one endless number line.** 6–8 themed theaters (Backyard → Sandbox → Garden Bed → Driveway → Rainy Gutter → Kitchen Table → Attic → Toy Store shelf), each **~40–48 numbered skirmishes + a boss** (RC tank, magnifying-glass emplacement, firecracker bunker, the family Dog). This is Battle Cats' chapter rhythm at Art of War's per-level speed: 45–90 second win-heavy auto-battles (Mob Control's density), with theater bosses as the Bun-Bun-style walls.
- **Difficulty via magnification + traits, not new art.** Re-use tan units at rising strength % per theater (Battle Cats' 100%→400% trick), and give tan forces **traits**: Armored (tin/diecast toys), Airborne (balsa planes, paratroopers), Swarm (plastic bugs), Dug-in (sandbag MG nests, mines), Elite (tan officers). Each trait is hard-countered by a canon pose (see roster). Walls every ~10–12 levels are tuned so the *intended* solution is "unlock/upgrade the counter pose and re-form," teaching the prep loop early.
- **Mob Control moment inside the lane:** the Molder's freshly-stamped squads periodically march through a **player-aimed multiplier gate (x2/x3)** before entering the lane. Idle production becomes a visible, active doubling moment — the game's signature clip.
- **Deployment grid meta (Art of War):** between battles, arrange poses on a staging grid ("the toy box lid") that **expands with campaign progress**. Formation placement matters (MG flanks, medic center, officer aura radius, sniper rear), and walls are partially solvable by re-arrangement — free difficulty depth, zero new content.

## Unit roster + unlock cadence (canon poses only)

Keep the classic nine as the entire roster — depth comes from tiers and variants, not roster sprawl. Suggested cadence (first week of play):

| Unlock | Pose | Role | Counters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | **Rifleman** | Core line infantry, Molder's default stamp | — |
| ~L8 | **Grenadier** | Arcing AoE | Swarm, Dug-in |
| ~L15 (wall 1) | **MG Gunner** | Sustained lane DPS, deploys prone | Swarm, Airborne |
| ~L25 | **Medic** | Sustains the line (heals "melting" plastic) | attrition walls |
| ~L35 (wall 2) | **Bazooka Man** | Single-target armor melter | Armored |
| ~L45 | **Minesweeper / Scout** | Disarms mines, reveals ambushes, speed aura | Dug-in, mine levels |
| ~L60 | **Sniper** | Long-range priority-target pick | Elite/officers |
| ~L75 | **Radioman** | ACTIVE: player-triggered mortar/air strike (the tap ability — Grow Castle's active layer) | everything, on cooldown |
| ~L90 | **Officer** | Aura buffs, expands formation grid, passive scrap bonus | — |

- **Rarity via plastic, not new characters:** each pose has mold-quality tiers — standard green → hard plastic → glow-in-the-dark → chrome/gold — obtained by **merging duplicate stamps** (Art of War's fusion). The Molder stamps duplicates constantly; merging 3 → next tier keeps the board churning.
- **Every unlock is a key** (Battle Cats): the level that walls you is beatable the session you unlock its counter pose. Losses print a to-do list, never a dead end.

## Meta layers (outside the battle)

1. **Scrap (soft currency, always flowing):** 5–6 parallel upgrade verticals with **out-of-phase cost curves** (Grow Castle): Molder stamp rate, plastic durability (all-unit HP), per-pose damage tracks, gate quality, base/sandbag defenses, scrap income. Rule: the player should never see zero affordable upgrades.
2. **Idle chest (Art of War):** Molder keeps stamping offline; scrap/parts accrue into a chest that **caps at 6–8 hours** and scales with campaign progress → 2–3 natural sessions/day (this is the D2 workhorse).
3. **Spare Parts treasures (Battle Cats):** every skirmish can drop a sprue piece at bronze/silver/gold quality; completing a theater's set grants a **permanent army-wide buff**; weekend "Parts Festivals" boost gold-quality odds (appointment + replay value for cleared levels).
4. **Melt-Down prestige as a content ladder (Egg Inc + LBR):** melting the army returns **Plastic Pellets** (permanent +% to everything, diminishing returns on hoarding so frequent melts win — Egg Inc's math). First melt reachable day 1–2. Each melt *milestone* unlocks a new subsystem: **Mold Workshop** (research tree, permanent) → **Recon Missions** (timed sorties returning gear, Egg Inc rockets) → **Arena** (async battles vs other players' saved formations, ~20 tickets/day — Art of War) → **Weekend Tournament** with rule mutators (no Molder / bazookas-only / double gates — Stick War's Crown of Inamorta).
5. **Daily loop:** daily quests → quest points → footlocker box (Art of War); rotating daily missions (Headhunt-style); 7-day login calendar whose day-7 prize is an early gold-plastic variant (gives D7 a face).

## D2/D7 retention map (what each system is for)

- **D2:** idle chest cap (come back before it overflows) + first Melt-Down available + daily quest chain + wall 1 (~L15) just cracked by the MG Gunner unlock the evening before.
- **D7:** login calendar payoff + first Parts Festival weekend + Arena unlock via melt milestone + wall 2 (Armored theater boss) resolved by the Bazooka Man — the player has now run the fail→prep→win loop twice and trusts it.
- **D30+:** theater treasure sets, chrome-tier merges, tournament mutators, and prestige-gated subsystems still unlocking (LBR's "what does THAT button do" pull).

The composed loop: *watch the Molder stamp → aim a gate, double the squad → win a 60-second skirmish → spend scrap on whichever of five tracks is cheap → hit a wall → the wall names its counter-pose → unlock/merge it → win → melt everything down and unlock a whole new system.* Every comp above survives on one of those beats; Plastic Platoon can run all of them on nine plastic poses and one Molder.
